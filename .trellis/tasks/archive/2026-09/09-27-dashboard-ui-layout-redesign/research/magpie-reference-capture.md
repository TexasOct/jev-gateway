# Magpie `#routing` reference capture

Source: https://usemagpie.ai/#routing. The page loaded in the managed Chrome browser at a 1200 × 1135 viewport. I inspected the rendered `#routing` DOM, computed styles, inline CSS, and changing states after selecting its tabs. The browser's visual outline did not provide a screenshot, so the layout and color details below are grounded in element geometry and computed CSS rather than an image capture. This live access supersedes the earlier access limitation recorded in `research/magpie-routing-analysis.md`; that file was left untouched.

## Observed section and composition

- The section opens with the eyebrow "Smart routing", the heading "The right model for each turn. It never runs dry.", and a paragraph explaining intent selection, per-turn model consistency, multiple accounts, and failover. An inline "How it works" link points to `/docs/intent`.
- Beneath the text is one rounded routing-demo container (`.rt`, 18px radius, 1px border). At the inspected dark-theme state, the body background was `rgb(11, 11, 12)`, the container background `rgb(22, 22, 24)`, and its border `rgb(38, 38, 42)`. Its top row holds five compact tabs and the selected mode's explanation.
- The desktop diagram stage was 990 × 375px, with CSS grid columns of 150px, 150px, and the remaining width, separated by 76px gaps. It places a `Claude Code` source card (subtitle "your agent") on the left, a `magpie` hub card (`localhost:3425` and a mode pill) in the middle, and a vertically stacked destination list on the right. An absolutely positioned SVG behind the cards draws one horizontal source-to-hub wire and curved hub-to-destination branches. The cards and rows carry icons, names, status text, and small dots. The hub has a stronger outline than the source card.
- In the default "Smart" mode the right column displayed five account rows: Claude Max, Claude Pro, ChatGPT Pro, OpenRouter, and Copilot Business. Rows showed usage/reset or payment information, thin usage bars, and transient status badges such as "serving" or "429 · rate limited". A bottom caption explains the current animation step, and three counters report demo requests, reroutes, and errors seen by the agent. Under the container are four short explanation blocks: "Reset-aware", "Out of credit", "Rate limits & quotas", and "Failover".
- The diagram is a directed branching flow with equal-width wires, not a Sankey chart. It does not encode traffic volume in branch width. The page's sample counters and account usage are part of its routing demonstration, not evidence of JEV production traffic.

## Observed interaction and motion

- The accessible tab list has buttons "By intent", "Smart", "In order", "In turn", and "Least used first". Clicking a tab updated its `aria-selected` state, the explanatory sentence, hub mode label, destination content, caption, and counters. The default selected tab was "Smart".
- "By intent" changed the destination column from accounts to four model rows: `claude-opus-5-5` as the first/default choice, `deepseek-v4-flash` for "a quick question", `glm-5.3` for "writing or fixing tests", and `kimi-k3` for "reviewing a diff". Its description says a small classifier chooses an intent once at the start of a turn and tool rounds stay with the chosen model. The demo caption and counters then tracked turns, classifier calls, and tool rounds kept on their model.
- The other three account modes updated their descriptions: "In order" uses the first account until it cannot answer; "In turn" rotates requests across accounts; "Least used first" chooses the account with the most allowance left. The tabs select presentation/demo modes. I found no evidence that the account rows or wires are editable controls.
- While the page remained open, the rendered account state, active wire, caption, and counters changed without further clicks. The SVG used a moving dot and a dashed active wire (`rtflow`, 0.7s linear infinite). The active destination dot pulses (`live`, 1.8s), source and hub briefly scale (`rttick`, 0.4s), a hit row shakes (`rthit`, 0.45s), and the caption fades/slides in (`rtin`, 0.4s). These are observed in the page's computed animations and inline CSS. A `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` rule disables those animations.
- The CSS switches the diagram stage to two columns at widths up to 860px and moves the destination list below both nodes. At up to 560px, account-row text wraps and the explanatory blocks become one column. This is stylesheet evidence; I did not separately resize and inspect a narrow live viewport.

## Boundary for JEV

The user's decision is to show configured policy only. Magpie's clear source-to-hub-to-branch structure, compact policy labels, and restrained branch tracing can inform JEV's diagram. JEV must derive its rule, fallback, label-pool, and model branches from its own configured draft. Do not reproduce Magpie's animated request counters, account utilization, rate-limit/serving badges, live-traffic implication, or branch volumes as JEV facts. If motion traces a configured route, label it as a policy explanation or preview, with a reduced-motion alternative. The observed Magpie section is a public illustrative demo; this capture does not establish its application editor behavior, internal routing implementation, or JEV runtime metrics.
